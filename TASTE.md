# Institutional / Risk-Advisory Brand Taste

Financial and insurance-advisory clients are not selling delight, they are selling **confidence under uncertainty**. The design's job is to make the reader feel the firm is careful, established, and precise — before they've read a word. Every generic AI-design tell (warm cream+terracotta, dark-mode+neon accent, identical rounded SaaS cards, ALL-CAPS eyebrows, arrow-suffixed buttons) reads as a startup pitch deck, which is the opposite of what an insurance broker, bank, or law firm wants to project. Actively avoid all five defaults listed in the frontend-design skill.

## Ground it in the specific firm, not the category

Before choosing anything, extract from the source material:
- **What does the firm's mark/logo actually depict?** (e.g. a compass, a swoosh, a shield, a knot). Let its geometry — not a generic icon set — seed the accent motif used in dividers, section markers, and hover states.
- **What is the literal meaning of the firm's name?** ("Kinetic" → motion, momentum, directed energy — very different from "Anchor," "Bastion," or "Meridian," which would each pull toward a different visual metaphor.)
- **What is the actual service sequence / structure in the source document?** Boards of directors, numbered duties, product schedules and risk categories are real enumerable content — use genuine numbering/structure for these, not decorative 01/02/03 badges bolted onto arbitrary sections.

## Palette

Reject cream/terracotta and reject near-black+neon. Institutional trust palettes are built from **one deep, saturated "ink" color** (navy, oxford blue, deep forest, aubergine — pull it from the firm's own mark if it has one) as the dominant structural color, paired with a **warm neutral paper/off-white** for reading surfaces, and **one restrained metallic or jewel accent** (a muted gold, a brass, a deep amber, or a single saturated brand blue) used sparingly for emphasis — never as a wash or gradient. Avoid rounded-corner soft-shadow "SaaS card" treatment entirely; prefer flat fills, hairline rules, and generous whitespace to signal seriousness.

## Type

Pair a confident serif for display/headline use (something with real editorial weight — Fraunces, Newsreader, Source Serif, Freight-adjacent — not the default Playfair-everyone-uses) with a plain, highly legible grotesk for body and UI text (Inter, Söhne-adjacent, IBM Plex Sans). The serif carries institutional gravity; the sans carries clarity and modernity. Do not italicize/color a single word in headlines. No tracked-out uppercase eyebrows. Set body copy at a generous line-height and under 75ch measure — this is read by risk officers and board members, not scanned by consumers.

## Layout & structure

- Lead with a hero that states the firm's actual value proposition in plain, confident language — not a vague tagline. A single strong statement + a real credential (registration number, years active, regulator name) does more for institutional trust than any hero illustration.
- Use a structured, asymmetric grid with clear section rhythm (generous vertical spacing, a consistent content column), not a stack of identical cards. Numbered lists (duties, products, risk categories) can use their *real* numbers from the source, set as large quiet display numerals — not circular badge icons.
- Bios (boards, leadership) deserve real hierarchy: name, credential letters, and a title treated typographically as more important than a stock headshot silhouette. If no photos exist, do not invent placeholder avatars that look like a generic person-icon grid — use initials or a quiet geometric mark instead.
- A regulatory/registration footer (RC number, license/regulator, address) is a trust signal specific to this category — always give it real visual presence, not a buried tiny-print afterthought.

## Motion

One deliberate moment only — e.g. the mark's own linework quietly drawing itself on load, or a hero statement that settles into place. No scroll-triggered fade-up on every section, no hover-lift on every card. Motion here should read as *precision*, not *playfulness*.

## Copy voice

Confident, plain, specific — write the way the firm's own documents write (registration dates, regulator names, concrete service descriptions), not marketing fluff. Prefer "We advise on the most appropriate cover and negotiate competitive premiums" over "Empowering your risk journey." Keep CTAs literal: "Speak to a broker," "Request a portfolio review" — not "Get started."

## Self-check before shipping

- Would this page be mistaken for a fintech startup's landing page? If yes, pull back the SaaS-card/gradient/neon instincts.
- Did every numbered element come from real source content, or did you invent a 3-step "how it works" that doesn't exist in the brief?
- Is the regulatory/credential information as visually confident as the marketing copy?
